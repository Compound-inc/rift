# Product

## Register

product

## Users

HR personnel who use Rift almost every day to filter and evaluate candidates for open positions. Their core job is moving through a stack of applicants and arriving at a confident shortlist. Teams collaborate with each other inside the same workspace, while org admins have access to specific configuration features (model, tool, and compliance policy, BYOK, member management, billing) to set the workspace up to standard. Predominantly web/desktop users.

## Product Purpose

Rift is high-performance AI chat infrastructure for teams, applied here to candidate screening. It exists to make evaluation feel instant: maximize responsiveness so HR users move through candidates faster and trust their decisions. Success is the user finishing a session thinking "I got through a stack of candidates fast and feel confident in my shortlist."

## Brand Personality

Fast, precise, calm. The interface should deliver a dopamine hit of momentum: snappiness you can feel, a sense that Rift is actively helping you move faster. Users should leave feeling accomplished and satisfied, not drained. Tone is confident and quiet, never shouty.

## Anti-references

- Enterprise heaviness (Workday, SAP): dense, slow, ceremony-laden.
- Cluttered, hard-to-navigate layouts that bury the next action.
- Generic AI-chat skins (ChatGPT-clone gray bubble dumps).
- AI slop: anything that screams "AI made that" template output.

## Design Principles

- Speed is a feeling. Every interaction should reward momentum: instant feedback, no perceptible waiting, motion that confirms progress rather than decorating it.
- Calm over clutter. Show the next decision, hide everything else. Density only where it earns trust (candidate data), never as default.
- Trust through clarity. Users defend their shortlist to others; the interface must make reasoning and state legible, never opaque.
- Structured, not stiff. Borrow Attio's record/table rigor and Notion's flexible low-friction surfaces, kept Linear-fast.
- Earn every element. If a component, label, or accent doesn't move the user toward a confident shortlist, it doesn't belong.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA baseline.
- Keyboard-first navigation (reinforces the snappy, Linear-style feel).
- Reduced-motion support honored via `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Color-blind-safe state communication (never color alone).
